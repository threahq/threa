Threa is a team chat with a memory. Calm by default, urgent when warranted, and built so the decisions made in a hallway thread on a Tuesday are still findable a year later.

Conversations, the way they actually happen.

Chat doesn't pretend each thread is an island. Boundary extraction notices when a discussion drifts, branches, or returns — without anyone needing to file it under a topic.

Station 02

Memory, not just history.

General Agentic Memory turns those boundaries into memos. The decision. The owner. The reason. The link back to the thread on a Tuesday in October. Findable a year later, not because anyone remembered to write it down.

A second pair of eyes on what the team knows. Surfacing context the moment it's relevant, never before. Not a chatbot: an unobtrusive companion that's actually been reading along.

Three moments you keep noticing.

Threa is built around a few small mechanics that compound. They're not features. They're the way the product behaves.

Boundary detection

It knows when a side conversation started.

Cheap models continuously trace conversational boundaries — even when people answer the wrong thread or move a discussion mid-flight. The memorizer only gets the pieces that matter.

Urgency, assessed

Calm by default. Urgent when warranted.

An urgency strip on the sidebar surfaces what's worth opening — red for a mention you can't miss, gold for an AI nudge, blue for activity. Most of the strip is gray, and that's the point.

Thinking spaces

A scratchpad with the team's brain attached.

Open a thinking space, write the question, get a grounded answer that references real conversations, memos, and links — not a hallucinated paraphrase. Solo-first; share when you're ready.

Vocabulary

A few words you'll see in the product. Calm, exact definitions — no marketing.

--gam
General Agentic Memory. A two-part memory system: a memorizer that traces what mattered, and a deep researcher that retrieves relevant memos when something needs answering. arXiv:2511.18423.
--memo
A small, addressable unit of meaning. A decision, a question, an unresolved owner — captured with a link back to the conversation. Memos are the atoms of the knowledge graph.
--boundary
The start & end of a thread of thought. Cheap models continuously trace where one discussion begins and another ends — even when humans don't help by staying in-thread.
--ariadne
The default persona. A second pair of eyes on what the team knows. Named for the thread you can follow back. Not a chatbot — a companion that's been reading along.
--urgency
An assessed score, not a setting. Surfaces what's worth opening. Most messages will score low — and that's the point.
--stream
Anything that carries messages. Channels, threads, DMs, scratchpads, thinking spaces. One abstraction; many surfaces.

A short manifesto · 2026

We're building the calm, knowledge-bearing chat we wished existed.

There's a phrase that's hung around our industry for a decade: "Slack, where critical information goes to die." We don't think that's a fact of nature. We think it's a design failure — and a solvable one.

01

Chat should remember.

The decisions a team makes on a Tuesday at 14:02 are usually the ones that matter six months later — and they're usually the ones that vanish. Threa builds a memory layer underneath every conversation. The decisions. The reasons. The owners. Findable a year later, not because anyone remembered to write them down.

02

It starts with one person.

The best team tools start solo. Scratchpads and thinking spaces are the front door — a place to think before there's a thread. Teams grow on top. A workspace of one should already feel powerful, not empty.

03

Calm by default. Urgent when warranted.

Color carries meaning in Threa. Silence is most of what you'll see. When something does earn a chime, an alert, a red mention — it's because the model and the rules agreed it should land.

"Don't optimise for engagement. Optimise for the moment someone reopens the app a week later and doesn't have to apologise for being away."
04

Ariadne is the thread.

Our default AI persona is named for the thread Theseus follows out of the labyrinth. Ariadne isn't a chatbot in a sidebar. She's been reading along. When you ask "did we ever decide on the Helsinki lease?" she answers in the only way that's actually useful — with the decision, the owner, the date, and a link.

05

Memory is a structure, not a search bar.

Underneath the chat is General Agentic Memory ({GAM}) — a two-part system that traces what mattered and retrieves it on demand. On top of that, a knowledge graph. The result is not "AI search". It's the shape of what your team knows.

GAM is recent academic work (arXiv:2511.18423) on giving agents long-running, near-loss-less memory. We've adapted it for the messy, async reality of team chat.
06

We're keeping it small on purpose.

Threa is in private alpha. We're manually vetting access while we learn — both because we want to talk to the first people who use it, and because a product like this earns trust slowly. Eventually this scales. For now, it's a small group of people we'd like to learn with.

07

Tell us what you'd want it to remember.

We read every email. Reply with what's been going to die in your team — even a single line is enough — and we'll get back to you.
