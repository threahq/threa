Iteration · v4 · 2026-05-24

Five blends of Vitrine and Ribbon — correct mark, AI demoted to plumbing.

Two notes shaping these: the logo is now the real Threa mark (a single vesica with a vertical S-curve thread, from apps/frontend/src/components/threa-logo.tsx — not the double-ellipse I had drawn before). And the copy stops leading on AI. The thing being sold is behaviour — conversations leave a trace, decisions come back, nothing slips. The AI is the mechanism that makes that quiet, not the headline.

Vocabulary shift “AI workspace” → “a quieter place to think”. “AI companion” → no companion noun on the home page at all; mention it once, deep in the page, as “a quiet helper that reads only your work”. The marketing surface talks about what stays, what surfaces, and what doesn't dissolve.

Direction

The brand mark, scaled up and threaded through. The ribbon enters from outside the frame, passes through the needle's eye, and continues out. Light cream page, Newsreader display, one accent word in gold italic. The dark element is the vesica itself, so the page's centre of gravity sits at the logo — not at a separate screenshot or panel. Cleanest possible expression of the brand metaphor.

Where it sings
  • The mark is the hero — no decoration to second-guess
  • The "thread through the eye" reads in one beat
  • Adapts well to About (lone vesica) and Dev (vesica with code threading through)
Where it hesitates
  • Hero leans abstract — no product moment to anchor the claim
  • Right-aligned mark may crowd long headlines
  • Needs careful tuning on mobile (likely stacks copy-then-mark)

Direction

Vitrine's dark frame, repeated three times — one for each beat — with the ribbon flowing through all three like a strung pearl. Centered, ceremonial. The three windows replace a screenshot: each shows what Threa does in one phrase, framed as an artefact. The ribbon ties them into one continuous claim.

Where it sings
  • Three beats are easier to scan than one giant statement
  • The ribbon becomes "the thread between them" literally
  • Reads as a single confident composition
Where it hesitates
  • Three dark blocks is the most visual weight of the set
  • Risk of feeling like a feature grid in disguise
  • Hard to scale to mobile without losing the strung-pearl effect

Direction

The whole hero is the vitrine — full-bleed dark, a single ribbon flowing through it — and the page returns to cream once the pitch lands. The dark moment is the front door; everything else is the conversation that follows. Captures the gravity of Loom while keeping Stage Light's restraint in the body.

Where it sings
  • The pitch happens in one breath, in a clearly-staged room
  • Light body keeps the page from feeling heavy
  • Strong contrast helps the brand mark and ribbon read instantly
Where it hesitates
  • Full-bleed dark hero is a commitment — alienates some audiences
  • The hand-off from dark to cream can read as "two pages stitched"
  • Needs the dark hero to be perfectly tuned; small flaws read large

Where to from here

The brand mark is now correct in all five. Copy de-emphasises AI — it can show up later as a quiet mechanism, not a headline noun. Pick a direction (or call out the bits worth combining) and I'll adapt it into About (longer-form why, contact form) and Developer (API reference, examples, recipes), then port the static doc into a real framework with live tokens from the product.

Worth deciding while looking:

  • Does the hero need a "product moment" (a small screenshot or memory chip) or is the brand metaphor enough?
  • Single dark element (01, 02, 04, 05) vs. three (03)?
  • Is the headline a single line, or three rhythmic beats stitched by the ribbon?
  • Should the working-hypothesis quote ("Slack is where critical info goes to die") sit on the home page, or move to About?
